# Changelog

All notable changes to Disrumpo will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [1.0.0] - 2026-11-10

### Added

- **Level meters** — The top bar shows input and output level meters (peak and RMS for left and right), each band's title shows that band's level, and a red light marks any sample above 0 dBFS until you click it.
- **Preset descriptions in the browser** — The preset browser shows the selected preset's description under the list, wrapped onto up to three lines (ending with "…" if it is longer still; hover over it to read the whole text), so you can read what a preset is built around before loading it. Every factory preset has one.

### Changed

- **Drive bites at normal levels** — The saturating, clipping, folding and rectifying types (Soft Clip, Hard Clip, Tube, Tape, Fuzz, Asymmetric Fuzz, the three wavefolders, Half Rectify, Temporal, Ring Saturation, Feedback, Allpass Resonant, Chaos, Formant, Stochastic) are now driven 18 dB harder into their shapers and brought back down by the same amount after them, so Drive 1 already distorts a -18 dBFS signal, while a signal in a type's clean range keeps its level. Bitcrush quantises on a grid 3 bits coarser instead (4 bits now crush like 1 bit). On a 220 Hz sine at -18 dBFS the default patch went from no audible distortion (-131 dB THD+N) to -19.6 dB, and every factory preset outside the Init category (116 of the 120) now distorts it by at least 5 %, where 56 of them did not before. Your own saved patches will sound dirtier, and heavily driven ones quieter, when you load them.
- **Oversampling up to 8x, chosen per type** — Each distortion type runs at its own oversampling factor: Tape, Temporal, the three wavefolders, Fuzz, Asymmetric Fuzz and Allpass Resonant at 8x; Soft Clip, Hard Clip, Full Rectify, Half Rectify, Ring Saturation, Feedback and Formant at 4x; Tube, Chaos, Granular, Fractal and Stochastic at 2x; and the digital types (Bitcrush, Sample Reduce, Quantize, Aliasing, Bitwise Mangler) and Spectral at 1x, where aliasing is the effect or the work happens in the spectrum. Oversample Max caps them and now defaults to 8x (it was 4x). At 8x one band of an 8x type costs about twice the CPU it costs at 4x (Tape 9.6 % -> 14.2 % of real time, the folders and fuzzes 1.6-2.1 % -> 3.2-4.0 %, at 48 kHz); set Oversample Max to 4x to save it. The reported latency follows Oversample Max (11 samples at 8x, 48 kHz) whatever types the bands use, so switching a node's type never changes it.
- **A morph between types runs at the nearest oversampling factor** — A band whose nodes ask for different factors runs at the factor nearest their morph-weighted average (a tie takes the higher), never above the highest factor among them. Four nodes of Formant, Chaos, Granular and Fractal at the morph centre run at 2x, and Hard Clip, Sine Fold, Full Rectify and Ring Saturation at 4x. This keeps four-node morphs affordable at the cost of some aliasing in such mixes: from a 1 kHz tone at -6 dBFS, Formant with three Tube nodes keeps its inharmonic content 74 / 58 / 57 dB below the harmonics at Drive 2.5 / 7.5 / 10, and Sine Fold with three Hard Clip nodes 62 / 59 / 50 dB. A node on its own, or a morph cursor sitting on one node, runs at that type's own factor.
- **Oversampling factor changes crossfade** — When a band's oversampling factor changes (a morph or modulation moving it between types that ask for different factors, a node type change, an Oversample Max change), the old and the new factor run side by side for 10 ms and the band crossfades from one to the other; small alignment changes that leave the reported latency as it is crossfade the same way. The new path's DC filters (and Allpass Resonant's loops) start from the state of the old path's, so there is no dip and no thump: a morph sweep stays within 0.3 dB of a straight line, and Sine Fold, Serge Fold, Triangle Fold, Asymmetric Fuzz and Allpass Resonant switch within 1 dB of their steady sound. Allpass Resonant's feedback loops still leave a short remainder at a switch, no louder than the input itself. The whole output dips for 10 ms only when the latency reported to the host changes (Oversample Max, or the delay of a Spectral or Granular node) and on a Band Count change. Each band keeps a second processing path ready for this, holding only the shapers a crossfade runs at the new factor: about 8 MB more memory per band at 48 kHz.
- **Allpass Resonant aliases less at high Drive** — It runs at 8x: at Drive 10 its inharmonic content sits 60 dB below the harmonics (63 dB at Drive 7.5). Above 4x its Damping keeps the cutoff in hertz it has at 4x, the rate its presets were voiced at, so they ring as voiced.
- **Less aliasing on Soft Clip, Half Rectify and Triangle Fold** — Soft Clip and Half Rectify run their shapers through first-order antiderivative anti-aliasing at their oversampled rate, and Triangle Fold's fold corners through second-order antiderivative anti-aliasing at 8x: at Drive 10 Triangle Fold's inharmonic content sits 64 dB below the harmonics (72 dB at Drive 7.5). At Oversample Max 1x they run without it.
- **Output safety clipper and the clip light** — An always-on safety clipper at the very end of the output keeps every sample below 0 dBFS. It leaves anything at or below -6 dBFS untouched, bends louder peaks smoothly towards -0.2 dBFS, adds no latency and no DC offset, and has no setting. The red clip light now shows when the signal reaching the clipper goes above 0 dBFS, so it lights when the clipper is catching overs. On a drum loop at -18 dBFS no factory preset peaks above 0 dBFS (Gentle Warmth reached +11.4 dBFS before).
- **Fuzz gets dirtier with Drive, not louder or cleaner, and the rectifiers stop getting louder above Drive 5** — Fuzz and Asymmetric Fuzz used to blend more of their clean input back in above Drive 1, and they and the two rectifiers rose in level with Drive all the way up (a -6 dBFS sine came out at +9.4 dBFS from Fuzz and +10.3 dBFS from Half Rectify at Drive 10). Now the fuzzes' clean blend falls back as Drive rises and their output is made up by 0.95 dB per unit of Drive, and above Drive 5 the output of Fuzz, Asymmetric Fuzz, Full Rectify and Half Rectify is scaled by 5 / Drive (3.5 dB down at Drive 7.5, 6 dB at Drive 10). At -18 dBFS the fuzzes' level stays within 1 dB from Drive 1 to 10 while their THD+N rises (Fuzz -10.1 dB at Drive 1 to -7.8 dB at Drive 10). Full Rectify's shape does not depend on Drive, so above Drive 5 its Drive no longer changes the sound.
- **Factory presets re-levelled, six re-voiced, one removed** — Each preset's Output Gain was set so that it is as loud on pink noise as it was before the harder drive (above), as far as its peaks (at most -0.3 dBFS after the safety clipper, on pink noise peaking at -12 dBFS) and DC allow; the four Init presets stay at 0 dB. 108 of the other 116 match within 1 dB, and 8 are quieter because their peaks or DC would not allow more (Pitch Drive 6.3 dB, Slow Scan 5.6 dB, Chua Circuit 4.0 dB, Allpass Shimmer 3.4 dB, Screaming Lead 3.2 dB, Alien Voice 2.9 dB, Fast Chaos 2.0 dB, Wide Sweep 1.5 dB). On a drum loop the presets are 2.3 dB quieter than before in the median (from 16.5 dB quieter, Ring Mod Lead, to 6.8 dB louder, Pitch Drive), and on the loudest ones the safety clipper rounds off the drum hits. Six were re-voiced to bite harder: Dirty Sub, Multi Dynamic, Asymmetric Bite, Digital Decay, Chaos to Order and Random Scan. Init 5 Bands, a five-band preset the four-band plugin cannot play, is removed, which leaves 120 factory presets (0.9.11 had 121).
- **Eight factory presets' sweeps now travel between two different sounds** — In Frequency Hunter, Formant Vowels, Ease In Morph, Inverse Link, Step Scanner, Wah Wah Drive and Wide Sweep the sweep morphed 17 bands between two identical settings, or towards a second node left at its type's default shape, so it did little or nothing; Formant Shift's four Formant bands were identical and its sweep stood still. Each of those bands now morphs towards a second setting of its own, as each preset's description says: Frequency Hunter's Tube, Sine Fold and Tape bands rise into Asymmetric Fuzz, Triangle Fold and Hard Clip; Step Scanner's three folders step to more folds and its Chaos top to a faster attractor; Inverse Link's octave Fuzz gains sustain and its Bitcrush drops towards 6 bits; Wide Sweep's folder, Soft Clip and Formant bands ease into more folds, a harder curve and higher formants. Formant Shift now gives its bands the vowels U, I, A and O, shifted from -12 to +12 semitones low to high, and a slow sine LFO moves its sweep so every band's formants slide about 4 semitones up and down. On a drum loop at -18 dBFS, moving one of these bands across the range its sweep covers changes the output by -29 to -2 dB relative to it, where Frequency Hunter's and Formant Shift's bands changed nothing before. On a 220 Hz sine at -18 dBFS each preset stays within 1 dB of its previous level (within 1.3 dB on the drum loop; for that Ease In Morph's Output Gain is 0.5 dB higher and Wide Sweep's, whose brighter Formant top raised it on pink noise, 1.4 dB lower) and its THD+N there is at most 1.4 dB lower than before (Frequency Hunter -2.2 -> -3.6 dB; Wide Sweep -2.8 -> 0.6 dB distorts more).
- **The band header's Bias knob only shows for types that use bias** — The Bias knob and its label in the expanded band header now appear only while the selected node is Tube, Tape, Fuzz, Asymmetric Fuzz, one of the three wavefolders or Ring Saturation, the types with their own Bias; for the other types the knob did nothing. It follows the node you select and its type.
- **Random, Sample & Hold and Granular start the same way every time** — The Random and Sample & Hold modulation sources, the Rungler and the Granular type now restart their random sequence when the plugin is prepared or reset (playback start, a sample-rate or block-size change, an oversampling crossfade for Granular). Before, each restart continued where the last one left off, so a project could sound slightly different depending on how often the host had prepared the plugin, and 17 factory presets sounded different after a sample-rate change. They now play a different, repeatable random sequence: 19 factory presets change once (for example Stochastic Burst, Granular Wash, Broken Radio, Stochastic Drift, Bit Mangler).
- **Output Gain applies after the Mix** — Output Gain now scales the whole output, dry and wet, so it also works at Mix 0.
- **Digital types no longer use Drive as a volume knob** — Aliasing, Sample Reduce, Bitwise Mangler, Granular, Chaos and Spectral divide their output by the drive above 1, so Drive changes the character without raising the level.
- **Fresh instances start with no morph smoothing** — A new instance runs morph smoothing at its displayed default of 0 ms instead of 10 ms.
- **Fractal Multiband bands sum flat** — Fractal's Multiband mode now phase-aligns its four internal bands, so they add back without the -0.8 dB dip around 320 Hz they had; its crossovers also start at their frequencies instead of sweeping in from 1 kHz over the first few milliseconds. Fractal Multiband sounds come out about 0.2 dB louder, mostly a phase change between 60 Hz and 1 kHz.
- **Crossover phase compensation in double precision** — The band crossovers' phase-compensation filters now run in double precision with an exact Butterworth Q and follow the crossover frequency while it glides. The dry signal at Mix below 100 % lines up with the bands to -140 dB or better at every sample rate (was -65 dB at 192 kHz), and moving a crossover no longer leaves a brief phase mismatch. The change to the sound is far below audibility.
- **Lower CPU use** — The oversampling filters, the morph engines and the distortion nodes do the same work with less overhead; Formant recalculates its vowel filters only when they change instead of on every sample; Fractal's Harmonic mode works out each level's harmonics for a block of samples at a time instead of one sample after another; and Granular no longer searches all 64 grain slots on every sample to find the few that are playing. The sound is unchanged sample for sample. Four bands of Formant nodes went from 19.3 % to 10.3 % of real time at 44.1 kHz.
- **Four-node morphs use less than half the CPU** — Ring Saturation now works through a block of samples stage by stage, Granular grain by grain, Fractal's Harmonic mode computes its default curve four samples at a time, Formant filters and shapes a block of samples at a time, and Hard Clip and Full Rectify run their curves in a loop of their own; the sound of each type is bit-identical. Together with the nearest oversampling factor (above), four bands of Formant, Chaos, Granular and Fractal nodes at the morph centre went from 35.1 % to 12.8 % of real time at 44.1 kHz, and of Hard Clip, Sine Fold, Full Rectify and Ring Saturation nodes from 30.1 % to 12.5 % (one core, measured side by side on one machine).
- **Built with VST3 SDK 3.8.1.**

### Fixed

- **The spectrum and level meters could freeze while the editor stayed open** — In a host that switches the plugin off and on (bypass, or after a preset changes the latency) from a thread other than its main one, the spectrum stopped moving and the level meters fell to nothing until the editor was closed and opened again. The displays now keep running: if the host stops delivering the plugin's display data for about a third of a second, the editor reads the audio directly, and it goes back to the host's path as soon as that delivers again. Switching on and off from another thread also no longer breaks that path.
- **The modulation rings froze along with the display path** — While the editor reads the audio directly because the host has stopped delivering display data, the rings on modulated knobs stayed at their last value. They now keep following the modulation there too.
- **Deleting the audio engine with the editor still open could crash** — While the editor read the audio directly, the spectrum read the audio engine's own memory, so a host that deleted the engine before closing the editor left the spectrum reading freed memory for up to a frame. The editor's displays now read only their own copy of the audio.
- **Switching the plugin on during a preset load could write into the running audio** — Some hosts switch the plugin on from their audio thread while a preset or project loads on the main thread. The load could then go on changing the audio engine after processing had started, which can glitch the sound or crash. Switching on now waits for a load in progress to finish; processing itself never waits.
- **A sample-rate change during a preset load could crash, and a save during a load could store a mix of two presets** — A host that changes the sample rate or block size on another thread while a preset or project loads rebuilt the audio engine under the load, which could crash the host or leave parts of the old preset running. A host that saved the project while a preset loaded could store part of the old preset and part of the new one. Both now wait for the load to finish; processing itself never waits.
- **Bitwise, Temporal and Ring Saturation pages could crash a host** — When their Shape page was rebuilt (a preset load that changes the type shown) or the editor closed, these types touched a control that had already been freed, which can crash the host or corrupt its memory. They no longer do.
- **Unloading with the editor still open could crash** — In a host that shut the plugin down before closing its editor, the update banner kept reading the deleted update checker every half second. The checker now lives until the editor is gone.
- **Moving a node's Mix could click** — Mix switched from the dry to the distorted signal from one sample to the next, so a jump of the knob or its automation stepped the output wherever the two differed. Mix now glides to its new value over about 5 ms.
- **All Types Morph went silent, and the Spectral type used far more CPU than it needed** — The Spectral type took its output one sample at a time from a buffer that it moved in full for every sample, so a preset with a Spectral node could need more than one CPU core: All Types Morph needed 127 % of real time at 48 kHz, more than a host can give, and played nothing. The buffer is now read where it is and moved once per FFT frame. The sound is bit-identical, and the presets with a Spectral node need far less CPU at 48 kHz: All Types Morph 50 % (was 127 %), Spectral Morph 7 % (was 90 %), Spectral Pad 10 % (was 57 %), Spectral Scatter 9 % (was 47 %).
- **The Tape type's Hysteresis model clipped, aliased, failed at Sag 1 and changed with the sample rate** — Its magnetic model overshot at every step and only a hard limit at the Sag level held it, so it made a clipped, square-ish wave with audible aliasing (inharmonic tones about 27 dB below the harmonics), went silent or turned to noise at Sag 1, sounded different at each oversampling factor and sample rate (a four-band patch with a Tape band was 1.4 dB quieter at 192 kHz), and its NR4 and NR8 solvers produced near-noise. It is now a stable tape model that sounds the same at every rate: a soft saturation with magnetic memory whose peak follows Sag, inharmonic content far below the harmonics, Sag 1 usable, and all four solvers sounding alike. Its character is softer and cleaner, and it keeps the dynamics of what goes in instead of squaring it off.
- **The Allpass Resonant node's Karplus topology was out of tune** — The string loop ignored the sample its delay line adds and the delay of its own damping filter, so it rang flat: from a few cents at A2 to over a semitone at A6 (2.7 semitones at the default Damping at 44.1 kHz). It now rings within 0.2 cents of the set Frequency with Damping at 0, and within 4 cents in every case measured (A2 to A6, 44.1 to 384 kHz, Damping 0 and 0.3); the worst is A6 at the default Damping at 44.1 kHz, a note that dies away in about ten cycles. The five factory presets with this node (Resonant Sweep, Feedback Explorer, Ring Mod Lead, Ring Chaos, Coupled Chaos) sound at their set pitch now.
- **A preset loaded through the parameters came back a hair off** — Loading a preset from the plugin's own preset browser, or a host restoring it through its parameters, rounded some values on the way (band gain and pan, crossover frequencies, node drive, tone and bias, morph smoothing, modulation amounts), so 53 factory presets held values a last digit off from a normal load, which chaotic and resonant types can grow into an audible difference. These now arrive exactly as stored.
- **Ring Saturation's carrier ran too fast with more than one stage** — With 2 to 4 stages the carrier advanced once per stage instead of once per sample (a 1 kHz carrier ran at 2 to 4 kHz) and the Track mode followed a wrong frequency (4.4-4.8 kHz for a 440 Hz input), so the type sounded different at each oversampling factor. The carrier now runs at its set or tracked frequency at every stage count, and an oversampling change no longer restarts it: the default Ring Saturation node kept its level within 0.1 dB across a 4x -> 2x -> 4x change where it dropped 15 dB. Ring Saturation patches with 2 or more stages sound different (the default 3-stage node about 8 dB louder at 4x, with a cleaner, lower spectrum).
- **Bias, Folds and Bit Depth had two controls that could disagree** — Each exists as a node setting and as the distortion type's own knob on the Shape page (the saturators' and folders' Bias, the folders' Folds, Bitcrush's Bits). A project load used the Shape knob, while automation could let the node setting take over, and in 76 factory presets the node setting a preset load showed was not the value the sound used. The Shape knob now always wins and the node setting shows its value; factory presets sound exactly as before.
- **Formant's Vowel setting did nothing** — The Blend knob alone chose the vowel (A at 0, U at full), so the Vowel menu had no effect. Vowel now picks the vowel, and Blend, centred on it, moves toward the previous vowel (left) or the next one (right) along A, E, I, O, U. A new Formant node plays exactly as before. Of the factory presets, 13 sound different, within 1.2 dB of their previous level on a drum loop and chord (Formant Shift in Morph and Alien Voice 1.2 and 1.1 dB quieter, Formant Vowels 1.1 dB quieter, Serge Bass 0.7 dB louder, All Types Morph 0.6 dB quieter, the rest within 0.3 dB); Formant Shift in Experimental keeps its four vowels exactly.
- **Modulation from the input ran ahead of the audio** — The envelope follower, the pitch follower, the transient detector and the sweep's envelope read the input as it came in, while every band and the dry signal play it later by the plugin's latency (about 11 samples at the default oversampling, 43 to 85 ms with a Spectral node). With a Spectral node a hit opened or ducked its band before it was heard. They now read the input as the bands hear it. Presets that use them sound the same except for that timing (level and distortion within 0.04 dB), apart from those whose Chaos type follows the input level: their chaotic patterns now unfold differently (Fast Chaos and Henon Map distort about 1 dB more on a held sine, Rossler Morph is 0.7 dB quieter on drums). Input Gain is the one destination that acts before the latency, so input-driven Input Gain modulation now trails the input by it.
- **The transient detector missed most hits and fired on held notes** *(shared DSP)* — It fired when the level jumped past a fixed point quickly, so it missed every hit of quiet material and most kicks at any level, and at high Sensitivity fired on every cycle of a held low note. It now fires when the level rises against the signal's own recent level, by 21 dB at Sensitivity 0, 12 dB at 50 % and 3 dB at 100 %, at any level above -60 dBFS, and fires once on a held note. The factory presets that route it now respond to 9 to 11 of 15 hits of a drum loop at -18 dBFS where they responded to 2 to 7; their level on that loop changes by 0.1 dB or less.
- **Ten factory presets had modulation that did nothing** — Envelope Follower, Punchy Low End, Temporal Edge, Snare Crack, Transient Shape, Touch Sensitive, Transient Punch, Transient Gate, Velocity Response and Multi Dynamic routed the envelope follower or the transient detector into a band's Mix (Velocity Response into the global Mix) that already stood at 100 %, so the routing could only push past the limit and changed nothing. Where the preset is about the attack (Punchy Low End, Snare Crack, Transient Shape, Transient Gate), the band now sits at a lower Mix (70 %, 60 %, 50 %, and 30 % and 50 %) that each hit opens to full; Transient Gate's detector also lets go over about 130 ms instead of 47 ms, so on a drum loop at -18 dBFS its gate opens on every kick and snare and closes again before the next hit. The envelope-follower routings push that band's Drive instead, by up to about 1 on a drum loop at -18 dBFS. Their descriptions say so. So that they keep their bite where those bands now sit partly dry between hits, Punchy Low End's Temporal band has more drive (2.5 -> 4) and so do Transient Gate's Soft Clip lows (2.5 -> 4.5): on a 220 Hz sine at -18 dBFS they distort about as before (THD+N -17.4 -> -18.1 dB and -18.6 -> -19.3 dB), though Transient Gate is 2.7 dB quieter there. Transient Shape is 1.6 dB quieter on that sine (THD+N -24.3 -> -25.2 dB); the others stay within 0.2 dB of their level and THD+N there.
- **The band header's Bias knob did nothing for types with their own Bias** — For Tube, Tape, Fuzz, Asymmetric Fuzz, the three wavefolders and Ring Saturation, the Bias knob in the band header moved a setting the sound no longer used. It now turns the type's own Bias knob on the Shape page: the two knobs move together, either way, and both change the sound.
- **Centre pan was 3 dB below unity** — Each band applied a mono pan law to its stereo signal, so with every pan centred the output sat 3 dB below the input. Pan is now a stereo balance: centre is exact unity, and hard left or right keeps the near side at full level and silences the far side.
- **Oversampling, Oversample Max and band Bypass did nothing** — The audio path never reached the bands' oversamplers or their bypass switch, so every band ran at the host rate and a bypassed band stayed distorted. Bands now oversample at 2x, 4x or 8x as their distortion types ask, capped by Oversample Max (see Changed), and a bypassed band passes its crossover signal through unchanged.
- **Tone, DC blocking and morph smoothing ran at the wrong rate** — Inside each band these were set up for 8x the rate they ran at, so a Tone of 8 kHz acted near 1 kHz and 10 ms of morph smoothing took about 80 ms. Each now runs at the rate it is clocked.
- **No latency was reported to the host** — Oversampling and the Spectral type delay a band, but the host was told 0 samples and the bands and the dry signal arrived at different times. Disrumpo now reports its latency, delays the faster bands and the dry path so everything lines up, and reports an endless tail while a Granular or Spectral node, or a Feedback or Allpass Resonant node with feedback above 0, is active.
- **Bands at different oversampling and the dry path now line up** — Bands were delayed by whole samples only, while the oversampling filters delay by a fraction (3.0 samples at 2x, 6.5 at 4x), so next to a band at another factor, or against the dry signal at in-between Mix settings, the sum dipped: -0.4 dB around 5 kHz and -1.3 dB around 10 kHz with a 2x band at 48 kHz. Each oversampled band is now delayed by the fraction that keeps those sums flattest from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (chosen per sample rate, and for a 2x band by whether a 4x band is in the patch). From 88.2 kHz up, Mix 50 % and every crossover sum stay within 0.02 dB of flat. At 44.1 and 48 kHz a 2x band without a 4x band stays within 2.0 dB (44.1 kHz) and 0.9 dB (48 kHz), the worst being at 20 kHz; in a patch with a 4x band the filters' own phase still leaves notches above 17 kHz (down to -17 dB at 48 kHz and -30 dB at 44.1 kHz), which no delay removes.
- **Left and right channels leaked into each other** — Both channels ran through one shared distortion and morph engine, so its filters and delay lines saw the two channels interleaved; a signal on the left alone came out of the right at up to -7 dBFS. Each channel now has its own engine.
- **Global Mix between 0 and 100 % notched frequencies** — The dry signal did not have the phase response of the band sum, so in-between mix settings cancelled around the crossover frequencies (down to a complete null with 2 bands). The dry path now carries the same phase response, and the mix stays flat within 0.02 dB.
- **Sweep changed the band's level instead of its drive** — Sweep Intensity multiplied the band's input, so it acted as a volume control, and it measured distance to fixed band centres. It now scales the drive of the bands near the sweep (up to double at 200 % on the centre), using the centres of the actual crossover bands.
- **Sweep LFO and envelope moved once per block** — The sweep LFO stepped once per audio block, the envelope follower heard only the first sample of each block, and a synced sweep LFO ignored the host tempo. Both now run every sample, and the synced LFO follows the host tempo.
- **Morph X/Y Link to the sweep worked only with the editor open** — The link was driven by the editor, followed only the base sweep frequency and was not saved. It now runs in the audio engine, follows the moving sweep and is saved with the preset; projects from earlier versions load with the links off.
- **One bad sample froze the sweep** — A single non-finite input sample stuck in the sweep's envelope follower until the plugin was reset, pinning the sweep centre at 20 Hz. The follower now recovers on the next sample.
- **MIDI learn did not work** — The plugin had no MIDI input, so a learned CC never reached its parameter, and mappings saved with a preset were lost. MIDI learn from a control's context menu now works end to end, and both global and per-preset mappings are saved with the project.
- **Hosts restored presets with the wrong type settings** — Loading a preset or project restored each node's shape knobs but not the distortion settings derived from them (threshold, curve, sag, tape model, feedback, vowel and so on), so a node kept the defaults or the previous preset's values. 115 of the 121 factory presets 0.9.11 shipped loaded wrong in a running instance; every preset now loads as saved.
- **A sample-rate or block-size change reset the patch** — Each change, and the usual host load order, rebuilt every band's morph nodes as a single SoftClip node and reset the crossover frequencies while the editor kept showing the loaded values. The patch now survives, and each band's morph mode and smoothing are saved.
- **Modulation routings and macros were cleared on activation** — Every time the host activated the plugin, including right after loading a project, the modulation matrix was emptied, and the next save stored it empty. Routings and macros now survive activation.
- **Sweep Width and Intensity reverted after loading a project** — The loaded values were overwritten by the defaults on the first audio block, and the next save made the loss permanent. Both now load as saved.
- **Presets saved from the plugin were stored wrong** — The preset writer saved the later modulation sources one entry too low, 1 or 2 active nodes as one more, the crossover frequencies on the wrong scale and a fixed custom sweep curve. Saved presets now hold what is set.
- **Old projects moved Sample & Hold, Pitch and Transient routings** — Projects saved before the Rungler source was added loaded these three routings onto the neighbouring source. They now load onto the right one.
- **Loading a preset during playback wrote into the running audio** — Preset and project loads changed the audio engine from the host's thread while it was processing. The new state is now handed to the audio thread and applied at the start of a block.
- **Morphing between distortion types jumped** — Morphing between different families stepped in level at 40 % and 60 %, two different types of one family switched abruptly at the midpoint, and in Linear 1D mode nodes C and D could never be reached on their own. Cross-type morphs now blend continuously, and Linear 1D visits A, B, C and D in turn.
- **Spectral morphs went silent, and shared counts drifted** — Morphing between Spectral nodes reallocated memory on every sample and silenced the effect, and two nodes that shared an FFT size, stage count or iteration count morphed to one below it (2048 became 2047). The FFT size now switches without allocating, and shared values stay put.
- **Granular drove grains five times harder than its Drive knob** — Every grain was driven 5x on top of the node Drive, and when a grain ended a neighbouring grain could be skipped or processed twice. Drive now matches the knob, and every grain plays through in order.
- **Stochastic made sound from silence** — Silent input came out as a DC offset, up to full scale with Jitter and Drift at maximum. Silence now stays silent, and Tube, Stochastic and the Temporal Diode and Tube curves now block DC.
- **Quantize turned silence into DC** — The level grid had no step at zero for many Levels settings, and Offset shifted the whole grid. The grid is now centred on zero, Offset skews only the decision points, and silence stays silent.
- **Fuzz could not be opened up with Tone** — Fuzz and Asymmetric Fuzz kept an extra low-pass near 1.8 kHz under the node's own Tone. The node Tone is now the only tone control.
- **Temporal and Stochastic nodes heard a Drive change late** — In a band with two or more nodes, a Drive change on a Temporal or Stochastic node took effect only with the next change to that band's nodes, so the last move of the Drive knob could be lost (a Temporal node stayed 3.5 dB quieter than its Drive setting). Every Drive change now reaches them at once.
- **Fuzz Bias below centre all sounded the same** — Every Bias setting at or below the centre clamped to the most gated sound. Bias now covers Fuzz's whole range.
- **Tube cascade stages shared one filter state** — With more than one stage, every stage ran through the same tube stage, so its filters saw the stages interleaved. Each stage now has its own.
- **The bottom fifth of Bitcrush Bits did nothing** — The knob ran from 1 to 16 bits while the crusher stops at 4. Bits now spans 4 to 16.
- **Feedback type: Curve did nothing, Lim off still limited, Threshold was reversed** — All three now work as labelled.
- **Ring Saturation jumped at Mod Depth 0** — At exactly 0 the output stage was skipped, so the level jumped at the end stop. Depth 0 now joins the rest of the range smoothly.
- **Fresh fold nodes were quiet and dark** — New Sine, Triangle and Serge Fold nodes started darkened by their Smooth setting, and Triangle Fold came out more than 20 dB quieter than the other types. Fresh fold nodes now start open, and the folders get makeup gain.
- **Clicks on common moves** — Changing Band Count, changing a node's type, moving Drive away from 0 and automating Input Gain, Output Gain or Mix stepped the output on one sample. Band Count changes now dip for 10 ms; a node type change crossfades from the old type's sound to the new one over 5 ms, with the new type's processor cleared (over 10 ms when the change moves the band to another oversampling factor, see Changed); Drive blends in from 0; and the gains and Mix glide.
- **Modulation left a band stuck at its last value** — When every modulation offset on a cross-type morph band returned to 0, the band kept its last modulated drive until the next edit. It now returns to its base settings.
- **Low crossovers drifted at high sample rates** — The crossover filters lost precision at low frequencies (the default 112 Hz split was 0.11 dB off at 192 kHz). They now compute in double precision.
- **Seven sweep controls and the node Bias had no knob in the editor** — Sweep Intensity, Falloff, Morph Link, LFO Waveform, LFO Sync, LFO Note Value and Envelope Sensitivity, and the selected node's Bias, were reachable only from the host's parameter list. The sweep row has a second line for them, and Bias sits next to Drive and Mix in the expanded band panel.
- **Node edits landed on the wrong node** — Switching a node's type kept the old type's shape values on the node, every node shared node A's per-type memory, and node, MorphPad and node-selector displays did not follow automation or preset loads. Each node now keeps its own values, and the displays follow the parameters.
- **Host parameter reset and display were wrong** — Resetting Band Count or Oversample Max in the host set 1 band and 1x, Sweep Intensity showed 0-100 % for a 0-200 % range, and many modulation parameters showed raw numbers (Env Attack 10 ms read 0.091 ms). Defaults and displays now match what the plugin plays.
- **Fresh Temporal nodes started in Derivative mode** — A new Temporal node now starts in Envelope Follow.
- **Type menus listed numbers before their names** — Several shape-slot menus showed nine numeric entries before the type's own options, and picking an entry chose the wrong one. The menus now show only their options.
- **The expanded band panel ignored Band Count** — An expanded panel stayed visible after its band was removed, or never appeared when the band came back. It now follows Band Count.
- **The spectrum analyzer showed spliced audio** — Its analysis window was pieced together from fragments of about half a second of audio. It now receives the audio contiguously at every block size.
- **Crossover dividers got out of order when dragged** — Dragging a divider between close neighbours could place it past one of them. Dividers now stay in order.
- **The editor took keys from text fields** — Arrow keys, Tab and Space were caught by the editor even while a text field or preset overlay had focus, so Space could toggle a band's bypass while typing. Those keys now go to the focused field.
- **The editor could crash on close** — Closing the editor after switching a band from its Shape page back to Main could touch freed memory. Fixed.
- **Modulation rings and the spectrum stopped after reopening the editor** — After the editor was closed and reopened, the modulation range rings on the sliders and the shared-memory spectrum stayed empty, and a host that connected the editor after activation got no spectrum. Both now work after any reopen or late connect.
- **The Morph Smooth knob sat under the Nodes buttons** — The knob is moved down so it is fully visible and clickable.
- **Preset loads re-applied each band's nodes once per setting** — A preset chosen in the preset browser sends 272 node settings in one audio block, and each one re-applied all four nodes of its band and re-routed their distortion settings: 272 times in that block. A band's nodes are now applied once per block, however many of its settings changed.
- **A project could hang on its second load** — A saved custom sweep curve with a middle point at the very start or end of the curve loaded once, but loading that state again into the same instance looped forever on the host's thread. Loading now always finishes, and curve points that are not finite numbers are skipped.
- **Crossovers could run closer than half an octave** — Only a divider drag kept adjacent crossovers half an octave apart; automation, the host's parameter list or a loaded project could set them closer, or out of order. The audio engine now runs each crossover at least half an octave above the one below it and returns it to its own setting when the one below moves away. Near the top of the range, where the upper limit (20 kHz, or 45 % of the sample rate if lower) leaves no room, the highest crossover takes the limit and the ones below it step down by half an octave each. The factory presets are not affected.
- **Chaos Speed showed 0 to 1 while the chaos source ran 0.05x to 20x** — The host's parameter list and automation lanes showed Chaos Speed as a bare 0 to 1 number, and its default read 0.048. It now shows the speed the chaos source runs, 0.05x to 20x, with its default at 1x. Saved projects keep their sound.
- **Solo and Mute clicked** — Soloing, unsoloing or muting a band added it to or removed it from the band sum on one sample, so the output stepped (unsoloing a band stepped up to 2.5 times the largest step of the steady signal). Bands now fade in and out of the sum over 15 ms.
- **Silence after loud material cost extra CPU in some hosts** — Decaying tails (a Tube node's DC filter, feedback and allpass loops) pass through the tiny "denormal" number range, which the processor computes slowly unless the host has switched that off; a Tube node's tail after a burst ran about 1.5 times slower. Disrumpo now switches it off itself while it processes, and restores the host's setting afterwards.
- **A broken input sample could reach the host's mix bus** — A NaN or infinite sample from the host came out of Disrumpo as it went in with 23 of the 26 distortion types (one band, Oversample Max 4x), and on a host's mix bus such a sample can silence or damage the whole mix. The output stage now turns any such sample into silence, and normal audio carries on from the next sample.
